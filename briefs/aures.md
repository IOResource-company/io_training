# Aures — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are)
Aures (now **Advantech-Aures**) is a French-designed POS hardware brand — compact, stylish, fanless all-in-one terminals and self-service kiosks, "made in France" heritage, now part of the global Advantech group. They sit at the **premium, design-led** end of POS hardware: build quality, small footprint, clean aesthetics and long product lifecycles matter more than rock-bottom price.

For IO Resource this is a **preferred, strategic brand** and roughly **#3 overall at ~14% of revenue**. The relationship runs deep — founding director **Tara Keogh** has 20+ years with Aures (and J2 before it), and IOR has a large installed base of Aures terminals across Ireland and the UK in hospitality, retail and pharmacy. Aures is also the front-of-house anchor for IOR's wider Advantech push into healthcare and self-service kiosks.

Product families to know: **Yuno / Yuno II** (compact AIO POS terminals), **Sango** (curved 15" modular terminal), **Nino** (entry AIO), and the **kiosk lines** built on the Yuno 27" engine — **Komet**, **Kwartz**, **Krystal** and the larger Komet variants — plus thermal receipt printers (**ODP 333 / ODP 444**) and customer displays.

## Product line (key SKUs)
- **Yuno II (ASC-415 series)** — Flagship compact all-in-one POS terminal and IOR's single biggest Aures seller. Form factor: AIO with tilting (0–90°) base, VESA/wall/pole mountable. Screen: 15" 4:3 (1024×768) **or** 15.6" widescreen Full HD (1920×1080), zero-bezel 10-point PCAP, 400 cd/m². CPU: choice of Intel **J6412 (Elkhart Lake)**, **i3-1115G4E / i5-1145G7E (Tiger Lake)** or newer **N150 / 3-N355 (Twin Lake)** — **all fanless**. OS: Windows 10 IoT, Windows 11 IoT LTSC 2024, Linux. NVMe M.2 (2 slots), TPM 2.0, dust/splash-proof front. *Use case:* the workhorse counter terminal for hospitality, retail and pharmacy where a clean, durable, fanless AIO is wanted.
- **Sango** — Distinctive **curved-design** 15" modular POS terminal (TFT 1024×768). CPU: Intel Celeron / Core i-series; 8GB DDR4; 128GB SSD; Windows 10 IoT / 11; USB / serial / Ethernet. Wide peripheral range, spill-resistant, multiple colours. *Use case:* front-of-house hospitality and retail venues that want a terminal that looks the part on the counter.
- **Komet (UTK-8200 series)** — Portrait self-service ordering **kiosk** built on the Yuno 27" engine. 27" Full HD (1920×1080) edgeless PCAP touch; Intel Alder Lake-N (N97 or i3-N305); 8GB DDR5; NVMe 256GB; fanless. Single-sided, double-sided or wall-mount; integrated **ODP 444 80mm printer**, **Datalogic 1500i 2D imager**, modular payment plate (Ingenico/Verifone/PAX/Worldline), optional 3-colour status light. OS: Win 11/10 IoT, Linux, **Android 13**. *Use case:* QSR / hospitality self-order and digital menu boards where floor space is tight.
- **Kwartz (UTK-8100 series)** — Slimmer single-sided **kiosk**, also on the Yuno 27" portrait engine; **made in France**, designed for single-technician install and global certification (CB Report). Intel Alder Lake-N (N97 / i3-N305); 8GB DDR5; 128GB SSD (to 512GB); fanless. Patented USB-controlled multi-voltage power supply enables **remote restart** of system/printer/payment terminal and door-sensor/status-light control. Wall, floor-fixed or free-standing. *Use case:* ordering kiosk for food-service, retail and leisure at a controlled price point with easy remote maintenance.
- **Krystal** — Slim, ultra-light interactive kiosk in **15" or 21"** (landscape/portrait). Intel Kaby Lake i5-7300U / i3-7100U / Celeron 3965U; DDR4 to 32GB; M.2 128GB SSD; integrated ODP 444 printer + Datalogic scanner + modular payment plate. Standalone, counter-top or wall. *Use case:* click & collect, queue management and counter self-checkout.
- **ODP 333** — Compact cubic **thermal receipt printer**. Direct thermal, 160 mm/sec, 72mm print width (512 dots/line), auto guillotine cutter, ESC/POS. **Triple interface (RS-232 + USB + Ethernet)**, **front paper exit** (splash/crumb/dust resistant), Energy Star (~30% less power), black or white, Windows 11 compatible. *Use case:* receipt printing in kitchens, bars and bakeries where splashes and debris are a risk.
- **TRX-3000** — 15" all-in-one modular POS terminal (1024×768; Intel Celeron / Core i-series; 8GB; 128GB SSD; Win 10 IoT). **Legacy model** — large installed base but limited parts availability; position **Yuno II or Sango as the replacement** for new deployments.

## Top sellers (what actually sells)
From IOR line-item sales (Aug 2024 → present, Aures brand ≈ €1.16m net in the window):
1. **Yuno II 15.6" (i3 Tiger Lake, 8GB)** — the runaway #1: **~€924k net across ~694 units**, roughly **80% of all Aures revenue**. This single terminal *is* the Aures business at IOR.
2. **TRX-3000-151 (J6412, 8GB, 128GB SSD)** — **~€100k / ~139 units**. Still selling into the existing base despite legacy status.
3. **Yuno II 15.1" (J6412 Elkhart Lake, 8GB)** — **~€40k / ~34 units** (the 4:3 / lower-CPU Yuno II variant).
4. **ODP 333 single-station thermal printer** — **~€26k / ~199 units** — the volume receipt-printer attach.
5. **Kwartz 27" kiosk (3965U, 8GB, 128GB SSD)** — **~€22k / 6 units** — kiosk projects are low-volume but high-value.
6. **Accessories/peripherals** that recur: Yuno II 2x20 customer display (~476 units), 10.1" rear customer display, Yuno II / Sango MSR modules, Sango customer-facing screens, fingerprint readers.

Takeaway: **lead with the Yuno II 15.6" Tiger Lake i3** — it is the proven volume product. Everything else is base maintenance (TRX legacy + parts), printer/display attach, or higher-value kiosk projects.

## Where it fits (verticals)
- **Hospitality** (cafés, restaurants, bars, QSR) — primary vertical; fanless + splash-proof front is built for greasy, crumb-y, wet counters. Sango and Yuno II front-of-house; Komet/Kwartz for self-order.
- **Retail** — fashion, convenience, specialty; clean compact terminal that suits a styled counter, plus customer displays and MSR.
- **Pharmacy / healthcare-adjacent** — strong installed base (Phil's pharmacy/GP/care-home channels); reliability and small footprint matter. Aures is also the front-of-house complement to IOR's wider Advantech healthcare display push.
- **Self-service / QSR kiosks** — Komet, Kwartz, Krystal for order-and-pay, menu boards, click & collect and queue management.

## Sales talking points
1. **Fanless = fewer failures.** No fan means nothing to clog with grease, flour or dust — a real reliability advantage in kitchens and busy retail, not just a spec-sheet nicety.
2. **One platform, many CPUs.** The same Yuno II chassis scales from energy-efficient N150 / J6412 for light retail up to i5-1145G7E for software-heavy or busy sites — easy to right-size and standardise an estate.
3. **French design + Advantech backing.** Premium build and aesthetics ("touch the difference") now sitting on Advantech's global manufacturing and supply-chain scale — design-led without being a boutique risk.
4. **Long lifecycle, big installed base.** Proven across Ireland & UK; IOR knows the platform inside-out (Tara's 20+ years), holds Yuno stock, and supports peripherals and spares from one source.
5. **Same engine, counter to kiosk.** The Yuno 27" powers the Komet/Kwartz kiosks, so a customer can standardise terminal + self-service on one hardware family and one support relationship.
6. **Future-proof connectivity.** USB-C with video alt-mode, 2nd-screen output, NVMe, TPM 2.0, Windows 11 IoT LTSC 2024 — won't be obsolete on the next software upgrade.

## Common objections + answers
- **"Aures costs more than the no-name fanless terminals."** → You're buying proven reliability, French design quality, Advantech's supply chain, and a platform IOR has supported for 20+ years — lower failure rates and a longer life make the total cost competitive, not the sticker price.
- **"It's only a Celeron/N-series — will it cope?"** → Spec the i3/i5 Tiger Lake version when the software load is genuinely heavy; light retail runs happily on N150 / J6412. Same chassis either way, so you're not locked in.
- **"We're standardised on TRX-3000 / older Yuno."** → That's the easiest upgrade we do — Yuno II is the direct successor, same family and peripherals, but current CPUs, NVMe, Win 11 IoT and current parts availability. TRX parts are getting scarce; plan the migration now.
- **"Fanless can't handle a hot kitchen."** → Fanless is *more* reliable in hot, greasy, dusty rooms, not less — the heat-dissipating aluminium structure and no moving parts are exactly why it suits food-service.
- **"What about kiosks, payment and printers?"** → All integrated: Komet/Kwartz take Ingenico, Verifone, PAX and Worldline on a modular plate, with ODP 444 printing and Datalogic scanning built in — one vendor, one support line.

## Competitive / displacement angle
- **vs NCR:** NCR is the incumbent tier-1/enterprise POS name, with the price tag, footprint and lock-in to match. Aures wins on **compact footprint, design, fanless reliability and value** for mid-market hospitality and retail that don't need NCR's enterprise ecosystem or cost. Position Aures as the right-sized, better-looking, lower-TCO alternative.
- **vs Posbank:** These two sit on opposite sides of the same coin for IOR. **Industry intel (and IOR's own Posbank positioning) holds that Posbank is an ODM that manufactures hardware for premium POS brands, Aures reportedly among them** — *treat this as channel intel, not a fact printed on either spec sheet, and don't overstate it to a customer.* The practical takeaway: **when the customer values brand, design and the established Aures relationship, sell Aures; when the customer is purely price-led, Posbank is IOR's value play at a comparable build.** IOR carries both, so the question is which side of the value/brand line the customer sits on — not whether to walk away.
- **General POS pitch:** lead with reliability (fanless, no moving parts), platform flexibility (one chassis, N-series to i5), and the single-vendor terminal-plus-kiosk-plus-peripheral ecosystem. Sell the platform alongside a software partner rather than competing on bare hardware price.
