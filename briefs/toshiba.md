# Toshiba — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are) — established brand, retail/logistics recognition, label-printer pillar.

Toshiba (Toshiba TEC) is one of IO Resource's preferred, strategic brands and a long-established global name in barcode label and POS receipt printing. They design, develop and build their own hardware, which gives them a reputation for build quality and a broad, well-supported service network across Ireland, the UK and Europe.

For IOR, Toshiba is one of the **two co-equal replacements for Zebra LABEL PRINTERS** — the label-printer pillar of the "Kill the Zebra" strategy, alongside Bixolon. The split is by customer fit, not vendor preference: **Toshiba leans toward retail majors, enterprise accounts, and any deal where brand recognition or service-network coverage is part of the buying decision.** Bixolon is the play where an aggressive price point is the main driver. Both are valid; the rep chooses based on the customer.

Toshiba's headline competitive strengths against Zebra: faster direct-thermal print speed, Ethernet standard (not a paid option), a linerless option, ZPL emulation for drop-in migration, and vendor-neutral SOTI Connect device management.

## Product line (key SKUs) — desktop, industrial, legacy

**BV420D Series (BV420D)** — *THE desktop direct-thermal model; the Zebra ZD421(d) replacement.*
- Print method: Direct thermal (flat head)
- Speed: up to **7 ips (177.8 mm/s)** at 203 dpi (5 ips at 300 dpi)
- Resolution: 203 dpi (GS02) or 300 dpi (TS02)
- Interfaces: **USB 2.0 + 10/100 Ethernet standard**; optional WLAN, Bluetooth, Serial
- Media width: 25.4–118 mm; max print width 108 mm
- Linerless variant: **BV420D-GL02** with integrated linerless cutter
- Emulation: TPCL native, **ZPL II emulation, DPL, SBPL**; BarTender UltraLite included
- Use case: the everyday 4" desktop workhorse — retail back-of-house, shipping/dispatch, healthcare labels, light manufacturing.

**BV410D** — *Desktop sibling to the BV420D in the same BV400D family.*
- Direct thermal, up to 7 ips, 203/300 dpi
- Same compact case and emulation set (ZPL II / DPL / SBPL)
- Differs mainly on operator panel (2x LED + 3x key with LCD vs the BV420D's 3x LED + 2x key)
- Use case: same desktop slot as BV420D where the richer LCD operator interface is preferred.

**BX410T (industrial desktop / thermal transfer)** — *4" thermal-transfer model.*
- Print method: Thermal transfer + direct thermal
- 4" class, ribbon-based printing for durable/long-life labels
- Robust metal-reinforced build for higher-volume runs than the desktop BV line
- Use case: warehouse/logistics and manufacturing labels that need ribbon durability (chemical, abrasion, long shelf-life).

**BA400 Series (industrial)** — *Mid-range industrial label printer.*
- Print method: Thermal transfer + direct thermal
- 4" industrial chassis, higher duty cycle and larger media/ribbon capacity than desktop
- Use case: continuous warehouse and production-line labelling where a desktop unit would be over-worked.

**B-FV4 (legacy desktop, still selling)** — *Legacy direct-thermal / thermal-transfer desktop.*
- Print method: Direct thermal (B-FV4D) or thermal transfer (B-FV4T)
- 200 dpi class, 4" desktop footprint
- Use case: still a live revenue line for existing estates and repeat orders; position BV420D as the modern successor on refresh.

**B-EX / B-SX (legacy industrial) and B-FV legacy** — heavy-duty industrial families (B-EX4, B-EX6) and older desktop/industrial units. Treat as install-base support and migration targets toward current BV / BA / BX models.

## Top sellers (what actually sells) — IOR sales data

From IOR sales data (ALL SALES BY SKU, Aug 2024–Jul 2025 window), Toshiba top SKUs by net revenue:

1. **B-FV4D-GS14-QM-R** — ~€14,966 net, **61 units**. Legacy B-FV4 200 dpi direct-thermal desktop. Still the volume leader — driven by repeat orders into existing estates.
2. **BV420D-GS02-QM-S** — ~€12,962 net, **52 units**. The current 4" DT 203 dpi BV420D (USB/LAN/RS232). The strategic go-forward SKU and the Zebra ZD421 replacement.
3. **6201-E65** — ~€8,436 net, **4 units**. Toshiba 15.6" bezel-less P-Cap multi-touch (POS display). Low volume, high unit value.
4. **6145-1TN** — ~€1,626 net, **6 units**. Toshiba 6145 203 dpi POS receipt printer.

Takeaway: desktop label printers (B-FV4 legacy + BV420D current) are the Toshiba bread-and-butter. The migration story is built in — point repeat B-FV4 buyers at the BV420D on their next refresh.

## Where it fits (verticals)

- **Retail labelling** — shelf-edge, price/promo, back-of-house. BV420D desktop; linerless GL02 for food/fresh and QSR. Brand recognition lands well with retail majors.
- **Logistics / warehouse** — 4×6 shipping and pick labels (BV420D for desktop/dispatch bench; BX410T / BA400 for durable thermal-transfer and higher duty cycle).
- **Healthcare** — patient/specimen and pharmacy labelling on the BV420D desktop; reliability and service coverage matter here.

## Sales talking points

- **Faster out of the box**: 7 ips direct thermal vs Zebra ZD421's 6 ips — ~17% more throughput on the same shift, visible in shipping-label and shelf-edge runs.
- **Ethernet is standard, not an upsell** — every BV420D ships USB + 10/100 Ethernet. On the ZD421 it's a paid option. Lower landed cost and simpler multi-printer rollouts.
- **Drop-in migration**: ZPL II + DPL emulation means existing Zebra/Datamax label templates, middleware and drivers carry over — no format rewrite.
- **Linerless option** (BV420D-GL02 with integrated cutter) — less media waste, lower consumable cost, cleaner bench; increasingly the standard in food/retail/QSR.
- **Vendor-neutral fleet management** via SOTI Connect — one console across mixed-vendor estates, vs Zebra's Zebra-only Print DNA.
- **Toshiba builds its own hardware** — service-network coverage and build quality are part of the pitch for enterprise and retail-major accounts.
- A **customer-facing "Toshiba BV420D vs Zebra ZD421" comparison guide is live on the IOR website** — use it as a leave-behind.

## Common objections + answers

- *"We're a Zebra house — our label formats and drivers are built around ZPL."* The BV420D has native ZPL II emulation (plus DPL). It's a driver reinstall and a config step, not a workflow rebuild — your BarTender label designs and middleware carry across.
- *"Zebra has more memory."* True — ZD421 ships 512 MB Flash / 256 MB SDRAM vs the BV420D's 128 MB / 128 MB. That matters only for very large on-device font libraries or extensive stored formats. Standard retail, logistics and healthcare labelling never approaches that limit.
- *"Is Toshiba a real alternative or a budget brand?"* Toshiba is an established global manufacturer that designs and builds its own printers, with a wide European service network. This is the enterprise/retail-major choice, not a budget compromise.
- *"What about healthcare-grade power or a battery?"* Zebra offers IEC 60601-certified and battery ZD421 variants. If a deployment specifically needs medical-grade power certification or untethered portable printing, flag it — otherwise the BV420D covers standard healthcare desktop labelling.
- *"Why Toshiba and not Bixolon?"* Both are our valid Zebra label replacements. Toshiba for retail majors, enterprise accounts, and where brand/service-network coverage matters; Bixolon where price is the deciding factor. Match to the customer.

## Competitive / displacement angle — THE KEY SECTION

**Toshiba BV420D replaces the Zebra ZD421(d).** This is the core label-printer displacement play (Toshiba side).

- **Spec wins over the ZD421**: 7 ips DT (vs 6 ips) — ~17% faster; **Ethernet standard** (vs paid option on Zebra); a **linerless option** (BV420D-GL02 integrated cutter) the ZD421 doesn't offer; and **SOTI Connect** vendor-neutral device management vs Zebra's Zebra-only Print DNA.
- **Technical parity = low migration risk**: same **BarTender** label-design workflow, and **ZPL emulation** (plus DPL/SBPL). Migrating off a Zebra is a **driver reinstall + a config step, not a workflow rebuild**. Existing label formats and middleware carry across.
- **Build-quality angle**: Zebra's ZD-series has known reliability issues since the GK420 platform move — clip breakage and roller-spring alignment problems are reported in the field. Toshiba, building its own hardware, is the more robust choice — a real argument in higher-duty environments.
- **When to lead with Toshiba vs Bixolon** (both valid — choice is customer-fit, not vendor preference):
  - **Toshiba** → retail majors, enterprise accounts, and deals where brand recognition or service-network coverage is part of the decision.
  - **Bixolon** → where an aggressive price point is the primary driver.
- **Asset**: the customer-facing **"Toshiba BV420D vs Zebra ZD421" comparison guide is already live on the IOR website** — lead with it, or send it as a follow-up.
