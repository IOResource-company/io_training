# Bixolon — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are) — Korean, receipt + label + mobile printing, strong print speed & connectivity, label-printer pillar (price-led).

Bixolon is a South Korean manufacturer of thermal receipt printers, label printers, and mobile/portable printers. It spun out of Samsung's printing division and is a long-established, globally respected POS and AIDC print brand. Its hallmarks are fast print speeds, broad connectivity (USB, Serial, Ethernet, dual-band Wi-Fi, Bluetooth) and a wide, well-documented driver/SDK ecosystem (Windows, Linux/macOS CUPS, Android, iOS, OPOS/JPOS, plus BarTender UltraLite for BIXOLON).

For IOR, Bixolon is **not a cold start** — it is an existing, live brand with an established baseline of receipt-printer business (the SRP receipt and SRP-S300 linerless lines have been selling steadily). Strategically, Bixolon is one of IOR's **two co-equal replacements for Zebra LABEL PRINTERS** — the label-printer pillar of "Kill the Zebra," alongside Toshiba. Within that pillar Bixolon is the **aggressive-price-point primary for the Zebra ZD421 installed base**: lead with Bixolon where commercial/price pressure is the deciding factor.

## Product line (key SKUs) — for each: model, what it is, specs that matter, use-case.

- **XD5-40 Series** — THE 4-inch desktop label printer and the designated Zebra ZD421(d) replacement. Direct thermal model **XD5-40d**, thermal transfer model **XD5-40t**, RFID variant **XD5-40tR**. Specs that matter: up to **7 ips (178 mm/s) direct thermal at 203 dpi** (5 ips DT at 300 dpi); 203 dpi (XD5-40) or 300 dpi (XD5-43); print width up to 108 mm; **multi-language emulation — SLCS native plus BPL-Z (ZPL II), BPL-E (EPL), BPL-D (DPL, optional)**; USB + USB Host standard, optional Serial, Ethernet, dual-band 2.4/5 GHz Wi-Fi, Bluetooth; 64 MB SDRAM / 128 MB Flash standard (optional 256 MB / 512 MB); EnergyStar certified. Use-case: retail back-of-office, shelf-edge and shipping labels, healthcare labelling, light manufacturing — the workhorse 4" desktop label printer.
- **XD3-40 Series** — entry-level 4" desktop label printer. Same printhead and motors as the XD5 but single feed button and lower spec. Use-case: **position as a value option only — NOT the ZD421 displacement product** (lead with XD5-40 for replacements).
- **SRP-350plusV** — 3-inch (80 mm) thermal POS receipt printer. Specs that matter: up to **300 mm/s print speed**; auto-cutter; USB + Ethernet + Serial; compact counter footprint; EnergyStar. Use-case: high-throughput retail and hospitality checkouts; a cost-effective alternative to the Epson TM-T88 class.
- **SRP-S300 Series** — 3-inch (76 mm) thermal printer available in standard receipt and **linerless label** builds; **splash-resistant**, built for kitchen/counter/bar. Connectivity USB + Ethernet (Bluetooth optional). Linerless eliminates backing-paper waste. Use-case: hospitality order routing and kitchen tickets; the SRP-S300 is IOR's top-selling Bixolon line by revenue.
- **SRP-275III** — 3-inch **9-pin impact dot-matrix** POS receipt printer, up to ~5.1 lps, 80 dpi(H)/144 dpi(V), triple-interface options (USB+Serial+Ethernet etc.), two cash-drawer ports via Y-adapter. Use-case: kitchens and back-office where heat, steam and carbon-copy/multi-part receipts rule out thermal.
- **SPP-R200III** — 2-inch mobile receipt printer. Specs that matter: up to 100 mm/s (80 mm/s linerless), 203 dpi, 48 mm print width; 1200 mAh Li-ion (~2.5 hr charge); **IP43 (IP54 with case), 2.1 m drop**; Bluetooth/Wi-Fi options with NFC easy-pairing; optional 3-track MSR card reader. Use-case: mobile/queue-busting receipts, field service, pay-at-table.
- **XT Series** — Bixolon's industrial/mid-range thermal-transfer label platform for higher-duty labelling. Use-case: heavier-volume label production above the desktop XD5 tier.

## Top sellers (what actually sells) — from IOR sales data (Aug 2024 – Mar 2026, net sales).

Bixolon total over the period: **~€68.5k net across 321 units / 79 line items / 19 distinct SKUs**. The revenue is led decisively by the **receipt + linerless** lines, with the label printer (XD5-40) now beginning to sell as displacement traction starts:

- **SRP-S300T (receipt, Ethernet+USB)** — **~€30,345 net, 119 units** — the single biggest Bixolon earner.
- **SRP-S300TX (linerless, Ethernet/USB)** — **~€14,228 net, 47 units**.
- **SRP-350plusV (USB/Ethernet receipt)** — **~€10,850 net, 62 units**.
- **SRP-275IIIC (impact receipt)** — **~€7,616 net, 40 units**.
- **XD5-40D / XD5-40T (4" label)** — **~€3,400 net combined, ~13 units** across several SKUs — small but live, and the strategically important number to grow.
- Plus consumables/spares: SRP-S300 cutter, platen, cover assemblies, and cleaning cards.

Read: the installed Bixolon baseline today is a **receipt/linerless business**; the displacement opportunity is to grow the **XD5-40 label** line off that trusted-brand foundation.

## Where it fits (verticals)

- **Retail:** SRP-350plusV / SRP-S300 at the till; XD5-40 for shelf-edge, price and back-of-store labels.
- **Hospitality:** SRP-S300 (splash-resistant, linerless) for kitchen tickets and order routing; SPP-R200III for pay-at-table; SRP-275III impact where steam/heat defeats thermal.
- **Healthcare:** XD5-40 for patient, specimen and asset labelling (note: no dedicated IEC 60601-1 clinical variant — fine outside strict clinical-contact environments).
- **Logistics / light manufacturing:** XD5-40 for 4×6 shipping labels, pick/putaway labels and WIP; XD5-40tR (RFID) for asset/stock UHF workflows.

## Sales talking points

- **Fastest of the three on the bench.** In IOR's own June 2026 Rathnew bench test the XD5-40 was the fastest on test pages — ahead of both the Zebra ZD421D and Toshiba BV420D.
- **Drops into existing label workflows.** ZPL II / EPL / DPL emulation means it slots into mixed Zebra / Eltron / Datamax / Honeywell estates with no label-format rewrites — same BarTender / label-design workflow.
- **Trusted brand, already in the building.** IOR has a proven receipt/linerless track record with Bixolon; the label line extends a relationship customers already know.
- **Open device management.** SOTI Connect (the MDM most resellers already run for Android handhelds) plus Bixolon's own XPM/XCM — no proprietary-only lock-in, no paid support contract required just to operate the fleet.
- **Connectivity breadth.** Dual-band 2.4/5 GHz Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Ethernet, Serial and USB across one simple line-up (XD5-40d vs XD5-40t — one decision).
- **Aggressive landed cost.** Bixolon is IOR's price-led label pillar — lead with it when commercial pressure is the driver.

## Common objections + answers

- **"We've always run Zebra / it's the safe choice."** Bixolon is a Samsung-heritage global print brand, already proven in IOR's own installed base, and it ran the customer's exact ZPL workflow on our bench with zero configuration. There's a live customer-facing comparison guide on the IOR website you can share.
- **"Will our labels and software still work?"** Yes — ZPL II / EPL / DPL emulation means existing label formats and BarTender designs carry over. It's a driver reinstall plus a config step, not a workflow rebuild.
- **"Is it as well built?"** Yes — and arguably better. The XD5-40 has cushioned/dampened roll holders (rolls can't slam back), more robust internal cogs, square-holding paper guides and one-screw service access. The Zebra ZD-series has documented clip/roller-spring weaknesses (see displacement section).
- **"It has less memory than the Zebra."** Standard config is ample for most retail/back-of-house jobs; for large Unicode font sets or many stored formats, spec the optional 256 MB / 512 MB upgrade at order to match the ZD421 baseline.
- **"No healthcare or portable variant?"** Correct — for strict IEC 60601-1 clinical-contact or battery-portable label needs, qualify the requirement; for the vast majority of labelling those niche variants aren't needed.

## Competitive / displacement angle — THE KEY SECTION. Bixolon XD5-40 replaces the Zebra ZD421(d).

- **Faster, more compatible, lower cost.** The XD5-40d runs **~7 ips DT at 203 dpi vs the ZD421's ~6 ips** (about 17% more throughput on the same shift). It adds **multi-language emulation (ZPL II / EPL / DPL)** so it drops into existing label workflows, and lands at a **lower / aggressive cost point**.
- **PROVEN at Intact.** In IOR's own bench test, an XD5-40 was driven live from **Intact Xline** — the direct-to-port ZPL workflow most of our reseller installed base runs — in place of a Zebra unit. The driver self-installed on plug-in, it auto-sensed ZPL on the port, **printed perfectly with zero configuration**, and was the fastest of the three. This is the headline proof point.
- **Technical parity / easy transition.** Same BarTender / label-design workflow and the same ZPL data stream. For most software (anything printing through the standard Windows driver) it behaves identically; transition is a **driver reinstall plus a config step, not a workflow rebuild**.
- **Build-quality angle (now photo-evidenced).** The Zebra ZD-series has a known weakness since the design change away from the GK420: spring-loaded roll holders drive a small, flimsy central cog that jumps alignment when rolls are slammed in, causing permanently offset printing — plus the clip breakage IOR already sees in service/RMA. The XD5-40's cushioned holders and sturdier cogs are more robust. IOR has its own bench photos of both.
- **When to lead with Bixolon vs Toshiba — both valid, customer-fit choice.** Lead with **Bixolon where aggressive price is the driver and commercial pressure is high** — and, per the bench test, **wherever the customer's software prints direct-to-port ZPL** (Intact and most POS estates), because the XD5-40 is a true drop-in there. Lead with **Toshiba for retail majors / enterprise / brand recognition**, or where printing runs through the Windows driver and the Toshiba brand/service network matters. Pre-qualify every displacement deal by asking whether the customer prints via the Windows driver or direct-to-port — it is the single gating question.
- **Comparison guide is live.** A customer-facing **"Bixolon XD5-40 vs Zebra ZD421"** comparison guide is already published on the IOR website — use it in the conversation.
