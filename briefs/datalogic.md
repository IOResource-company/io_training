# Datalogic — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are)

Datalogic is an Italian AIDC (Automatic Identification & Data Capture) specialist with one unifying discipline: **everything they make captures data — nothing prints it.** The portfolio spans five pillars: **fixed retail scanners** (the Magellan family — **#1 global market leader in both bioptic and single-plane scanners**, plus the 900i/1500i presentation scanners and GFS4500 OEM/kiosk engines), **handheld scanners** (QuickScan, Gryphon, PowerScan — around #2 globally), **mobile computers** (Memor rugged Android PDAs, Skorpio keyed/pistol-grip, Joya Smart self-shopping), **stationary industrial scanners** (Matrix 320, AV900), and **sensors, safety & machine vision** for factory automation. Scale: roughly 2,900 employees, $520M global sales, 30 offices worldwide, 1,200+ patents. They explicitly do **not** make printers and have no plans to; they partner with print vendors instead. Their four focus verticals are **retail (biggest), transport & logistics, manufacturing, and healthcare.**

**IO Resource is a Datalogic Platinum Partner for UK & Ireland (agreement signed 2026).** Datalogic sells only through the channel — there is always a partner in the middle — which fits IOR's arm's-length, value-add distribution model. The McDonald's self-service kiosk scanner, for example, is typically a Datalogic unit.

**Strategic role:** Datalogic is IOR's **preferred brand and the strategic lead** for scanning. It is the **primary replacement for Zebra barcode scanners** — the scanner pillar of the "Kill the Zebra" displacement strategy. When a rep is replacing a Zebra scanner, the default answer is Datalogic.

## Product line (key SKUs)

**Magellan 900i — THE lead presentation scanner (Zebra DS9308 replacement)**
- *What it is:* Compact omnidirectional 1D/2D presentation scanner for retail and service counters.
- *Specs that matter:* 1 MP imager; reads 1D/2D from print **and mobile phone screens**; LED aimer for PLU lists/small items; Green Spot good-read feedback; 1.2 m drop; IP52; 3-year warranty; Checkpoint EAS compatible; OPOS/JavaPOS/Aladdin remote management.
- *Use case:* The everyday till/counter scanner — this is what IOR actually sells and demos against the DS9308.

**Magellan 1500i — premium step-up presentation scanner**
- *What it is:* Stylish omnidirectional 1D/2D presentation scanner with an aluminium base and indicator strip for a premium, customer-facing finish.
- *Specs that matter:* 1 MP imager; reads on-screen mobile coupons; optional Digimarc decoding; black or white enclosures; multiple mounts (tilting riser, fixed stand, wall, magnetic base); wide reading angles (±75° skew); 25 kV ESD; 1.2 m drop; IP52; 3-year warranty.
- *Use case:* Premium tills and brand-conscious counters where finish and aesthetics matter — the up-sell from the 900i.

**QuickScan (QD2500 / QD2590 / QW2520) — value handheld imager**
- *What it is:* Affordable 1D/2D handheld imager for retail POS and back-office.
- *Specs that matter:* 1 MP imager; Motionix motion-sensing auto-switch between handheld and presentation modes; reads through plexiglass barriers and from mobile screens; USB-C cable option; 1.5 m drop; IP52; 5-year warranty.
- *Use case:* General-purpose corded scanning at the till and stockroom on a tight budget.

**Gryphon — premium general-purpose handheld (mid-range line)**
- *What it is:* Datalogic's premium general-purpose handheld/presentation imager family, a step above QuickScan.
- *Specs that matter:* High-performance 1D/2D omnidirectional decoding; corded and cordless (Bluetooth) options; Green Spot feedback; auto-stand presentation modes; healthcare-disinfectant variants available.
- *Use case:* Retail and healthcare counters wanting a more capable, better-built handheld than entry value lines.

**PowerScan — rugged industrial handheld**
- *What it is:* Datalogic's rugged scanner line for warehouse, DC and manufacturing.
- *Specs that matter:* Sealed industrial housing (high IP rating); tough drop/tumble spec; standard, high-density and long-range optics; corded and cordless models; built for cold/harsh environments.
- *Use case:* Warehouse goods-in, picking, and shop-floor scanning where ruggedness matters most.

**Memor (12 / 17 / 30-35) — enterprise Android mobile computers**
- *What it is:* Slim enterprise Android PDAs with integrated 2D scanning for retail, T&L and healthcare.
- *Specs that matter:* 6" FHD display; Android 13 (roadmap through Android 19); Wi-Fi 6E + optional 5G; Halogen 2121DL scan engine with Green Spot and DeepSight (reads damaged/dense codes); MIL-STD 810H, IP65/IP67; 4+4 year hardware/software support. (Memor 17 HC adds a disinfectant-ready chassis and alarm button for clinical use; Memor 30/35 adds extended-range scanning up to 10 m.)
- *Use case:* Mobile workers needing a managed, rugged Android handheld computer — store associates, warehouse, clinical staff.

*(Adjacent Datalogic mobility on the IOR range: Skorpio X5 rugged pistol-grip computer and the Joya Smart AI personal-shopping device.)*

## Sales talking points

- **Magellan 900i is the lead product.** It is what IOR stocks, demos and quotes by default for presentation/counter scanning. Lead with the 900i in every till conversation.
- **Magellan 1500i is the premium step-up.** Aluminium base and indicator strip give a polished, customer-facing finish. Use it as the up-sell where aesthetics and a premium counter image matter — Zebra has no equivalent step-up under the DS9308.
- **Phone-screen scanning works out of the box** on both Magellans — increasingly a hard till requirement for mobile coupons and loyalty.
- **Green Spot good-read feedback** is well-liked by operators and reduces scan errors.
- **One vendor, one discipline — data capture:** Datalogic makes everything that reads a barcode, from till scanner to bioptic lane to rugged Android handheld — and nothing that prints one. They are #1 globally in bioptic and single-plane retail scanners.
- **Service-backed:** 3-year warranty on presentation scanners, 5-year on QuickScan; IOR holds stock, does in-house repairs and offers next-day delivery.

## Competitive / displacement angle — KEY SECTION

**The headline mapping: Datalogic Magellan 900i replaces the Zebra DS9308.** The DS9308 is IOR's single biggest scanner displacement target, and the 900i is the like-for-like replacement we put in front of customers. Use the following six points from the live three-way bench test — these are the practical, out-of-the-box behaviours that win at a real till, regardless of what a spec sheet says.

**The six bench-test sales points (use against the DS9308):**

1. **Faster trigger & decode.** Datalogic's proximity sensor is highly sensitive — walk anywhere near it and it triggers. The Zebra typically needs a deliberate present-and-hold motion.
2. **Phone-screen / mobile-coupon scanning works out of the box** on Datalogic — a common, often non-negotiable till requirement. No reconfiguration needed.
3. **On-device configuration.** Change settings on the unit itself: **hold 2 seconds = volume up/down via the rocker; hold 4 seconds = factory reset.** Zebra requires the 123Scan Windows utility — a software install, admin rights and a USB cable.
4. **Carriage return enabled by default.** Every Zebra customer asks how to turn this on; Datalogic ships with it already on.
5. **More interface options.** Datalogic supports USB-HID, serial, and **virtual serial COM**. Zebra is USB or serial only.
6. **Premium step-up within the line.** The Magellan 1500i (aluminium base, indicator strip) gives a customer-facing premium finish. Zebra has no equivalent step-up under the DS9308.

**Honest framing for reps:** On a pure spec sheet the DS9308 can look strong on some lab metrics (minimum print contrast, drop/tumble spec, OCR, software ecosystem). The Datalogic case is won on **real-till behaviour and out-of-the-box readiness** — trigger speed, phone screens, carriage return on by default, on-device config without a Windows utility — plus the premium 1500i step-up. Steer the conversation to a side-by-side bench test with the customer's own barcodes and labels; that is where the Datalogic experience shows.

**Deployment note (add to the pre-deployment checklist):** Dense / Kanji QR codes are **OFF by default on Datalogic as a security setting.** If a customer needs them, fix it by **scanning three barcodes from the quick-start guide.** Always check this before handover so it isn't mistaken for a fault.

## Common objections + answers

- **"We've always used Zebra."** Fair — and the Magellan 900i is the like-for-like replacement for the DS9308. In a live bench test it triggers faster (proximity sensor vs present-and-hold), reads phone screens out of the box, and ships with carriage return already on — the three things that actually slow staff down at the till.

- **"Our team knows Zebra; retraining is a hassle."** There's almost nothing to retrain. Config is done **on the device** — hold 2 seconds for volume, 4 seconds for a factory reset — instead of installing the 123Scan Windows utility with admin rights and a USB cable. For most settings, no PC tool is needed at all.

- **"Zebra has the bigger software ecosystem."** True for large estates managing scanners at scale — but most retail tills never touch that tooling. What matters day-to-day is that Datalogic works out of the box: carriage return on, phone-screen scanning enabled, and more interface options (USB-HID, serial, virtual serial COM) than Zebra's USB/serial-only.

- **"Is Datalogic a serious brand?"** They're the #1 global market leader in bioptic and single-plane retail scanners — roughly $520M in global sales, 2,900 employees and 1,200+ patents. The kiosk scanner at McDonald's is typically Datalogic. IOR is a Datalogic Platinum Partner for UK & Ireland and backs it with stock, in-house repairs and next-day delivery.

- **"We need a premium look at customer-facing tills."** That's the Magellan 1500i — aluminium base and indicator strip. Zebra has no equivalent step-up under the DS9308.
