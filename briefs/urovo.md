# Urovo — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are)

Urovo is a Chinese AIDC (automatic identification and data capture) manufacturer specialising in rugged Android mobile computers, handheld terminals, smart POS devices, RFID readers, and wearable/ring scanners. In 2022–2023 Urovo ranked **No. 2 globally for rugged handheld computers** by volume, and its European reference customers include **DHL, Uber, Carrefour, Hermes, Deutsche Post, and Tesco** — credentials that matter when an enterprise buyer asks "who else runs these?"

The range is built on current Qualcomm silicon, ships with **Google Mobile Services (GMS) certification** and **Android Enterprise Recommended (AER)** status on the lead models, and supports OTA management through standard MDM platforms (SOTI MobiControl, SOTI XSight, etc.). Build quality, battery life, and competitive pricing are the consistent themes across the line.

IOR carries Urovo because it is a credible, well-priced enterprise-mobility brand that we hold in **local Irish/UK stock** with a clean **warranty/RMA path** — and, strategically, because Urovo is **IOR's primary replacement for Zebra handheld computers**. Within the "Kill the Zebra" displacement plan, the three pillars are scanners (Datalogic), label printers (Bixolon/Toshiba), and **handhelds (Urovo, lead brand)**. Urovo is the first-priority answer for displacing the Zebra TC and MC mobile-computer ranges. Urovo is ~8.5% of IOR revenue.

## Product line (key SKUs)

> Spec note: the **DT50S, RT40/RT40S, DT66, and P8100P** are the current-generation devices and carry the newest Android builds. Older DT40 / DT50 / CT-series stock ships on earlier Android with an upgrade path — always confirm the exact build on the unit being quoted (see objections + the LTS/accessory validation note below).

**Urovo RT40 / RT40S — rugged industrial handheld (flagship)**
- What it is: full-rugged keypad/touch industrial mobile computer; the bridge device between consumer and heavy-rugged, and the answer to warehouse pistol-grip and heavy-warehouse Zebra models.
- Android: **13 (GMS / AER), stated upgrade path to Android 17** · Processor: Qualcomm octa-core 2.45 GHz · RAM/storage: 4 GB / 64 GB (8 GB / 128 GB option) · Scan engine: standard-range and **extended-range to 15 m** options · IP rating: **IP68**, 1.8 m drop to concrete · 4G: integrated 4G/3G/2G · Battery: **5,200 mAh hot-swap** (no reboot); optional **−30 °C cold-chain variant** · Keypad options 29/38/51 keys · Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) 2×2 MU-MIMO.
- Use case: warehouse pick/put-away, high-bay racking, cold-store/freezer, yard and field — the toughest environments.

**Urovo DT40 — compact 4" rugged handheld**
- What it is: compact, light rugged handheld with physical keypad; the main everyday TC-range answer.
- Android: 9 / 12 depending on build (**confirm per unit**) · Processor: octa-core ~1.8 GHz · RAM/storage: 2–3 GB / 16–32 GB, microSD to 256 GB · Scan engine: professional 1D/2D · IP rating: **IP67**, 1.5 m drop to concrete · 4G: dual-SIM 4G/3G/2G · Battery: 4,500 mAh · Wi-Fi 5.
- Use case: retail in-store, warehouse picking, field service; takes a **gun grip** for pistol-grip workflows.

**Urovo DT50 / DT50S — 5.7" rugged enterprise handheld (mid-tier)**
- What it is: larger-screen enterprise handheld; the mid-tier workhorse and the proven mPOS-credible platform (Dreamtec bakery rollout, below). DT50S is the current scan-focused variant.
- Android: **DT50S = Android 13 (GMS)** · Processor: Qualcomm SD680 (SD660 on older DT50) · RAM/storage: 4 GB / 64 GB, microSD to 256 GB · Scan engine: professional 1D/2D (SE4770-class) · IP rating: **IP67** · 4G: 4G LTE · Battery: 4,500 mAh · Wi-Fi 6, BT 5.1, NFC. Variants: **DT50P** adds Impinj E710 **UHF RFID** + pistol grip (>20 m read, 9,000 mAh); a no-scanner DT50S variant is the lowest price point.
- Use case: retail, warehouse picking, direct-store-delivery, mPOS/tap-to-pay.

**Other notable models IOR sells**
- **DT66** — current-gen 6" handheld, **Android 14 (GMS)**, IP68, Wi-Fi 6 — strong value alternative; the newest mainstream model.
- **P8100P / P8100H** — 10.1" rugged Android tablets. P8100P: **Android 13, 5G + Wi-Fi 6E, 10,000 mAh**. P8100H: antibacterial housing for healthcare/food.
- **CT48 / CT58S** — compact rugged handhelds with physical keypad (Android 11/12).
- **R70 / U2** — wearable 2D ring scanners for hands-free picking.
- **FR1000 / FR-series** — fixed/programmable UHF RFID readers.
- **K180 / K329 / K419** — Bluetooth scanner and mobile printers (printer ZPL caveat below).

## Sales talking points

1. **Current Android with a real upgrade runway.** Lead models ship on **Android 13 (GMS/AER)** — RT40/RT40S has a stated path to **Android 17**, DT66 is already on Android 14. For a new 4–5-year deployment that puts the customer near the front of the support cycle, not the back, with ongoing security patches.
2. **Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) with 2×2 MU-MIMO** as standard on the current range — matches the Wi-Fi 6 / 6E infrastructure now being rolled across DCs and stores.
3. **Best-in-class ruggedisation** — **IP68** on RT40S (dust-tight, sustained submersion) and IP67 across the mainstream range, 1.5–1.8 m drop to concrete, plus a true **−30 °C cold-chain** RT40S variant.
4. **Integrated 4G** across the handheld line — useful for yard, kerbside, DSD and field where Wi-Fi doesn't reach.
5. **mPOS credibility** — proven in the field: IOR supplied **280 × Urovo DT50** to **Dreamtec Systems** for a phased 12–14 month nationwide bakery rollout (delivery drivers + warehouse, SOTI MDM-managed, tap-to-pay and scanning).
6. **Strong price/warranty position** — competitive device pricing, **local Irish/UK stock with flexible call-off**, and multi-year comprehensive cover (e.g. DT40COM3YR) on an Irish-based RMA path — no international lead-time risk.
7. **Hot-swap battery** on RT40S completes a battery change **without a reboot** — full shifts with no downtime.

## Common objections + answers

**"What Android version is it, and how long will it get security patches?"**
Lead models ship on **Android 13 with GMS and AER**; RT40/RT40S has a stated upgrade path to **Android 17** and DT66 is on **Android 14**. Confirm the patch/LTS commitment for the specific model before pitching enterprise — validate Android LTS (13/14) with quarterly security patches per model (this is the standard pre-pitch gate).

**"Is the accessory ecosystem there — cradles, gun grip, vehicle mount?"**
Yes — single- and multi-slot charging cradles (HBCDT40, HBCRT40, HBCDT50B), pistol/gun grips, holsters, armband holsters, vehicle cradles, and hand straps are all stocked. **Validate accessory parity for the exact model** before quoting an enterprise account, as availability varies by device generation.

**"How mature is the SDK / developer tooling?"**
Urovo ships a standard Android SDK for scan-engine and device control, GMS certification for broad app compatibility, and works with mainstream MDM (SOTI MobiControl/XSight). For most barcode and line-of-business apps it's a drop-in. Pre-qualify any deeply customised integration. Note specifically: **Urovo mobile printers (SP319/409, K-series) need SDK work for ZPL** — see displacement section.

**"We're a Zebra shop — nobody's heard of Urovo."**
Urovo was **No. 2 globally for rugged handhelds (2022–23)**, runs at DHL, Carrefour, Tesco, Hermes, Deutsche Post and Uber, and we have a live 280-unit Irish deployment (Dreamtec) and a published RT40S-vs-MC3300x comparison. Where customer-facing brand recognition is critical or extended-range scanning above 15 m is a hard requirement, we'll flag it honestly — but for the large majority of TC/MC workflows the Urovo is the forward-looking, better-value platform.

## Competitive / displacement angle

This is the key section. Urovo is **IOR's primary Zebra-handheld replacement.** Map the customer's existing Zebra TC/MC model to the Urovo equivalent:

| Zebra model | Typical use | Urovo replacement |
|---|---|---|
| TC15 / TC16 | Consumer retail, scan & go | **RT40** |
| TC21 / TC22 | In-store, clienteling, mPOS | **RT40 / DT40** |
| TC26 / TC27 (+4G) | TC21/22 with cellular | **DT40 4G / RT40 4G** |
| TC52 / TC57 / TC58 | Retail, warehouse picking (mid-tier) | **DT50** (adds mPOS credibility) |
| MC3300 / MC3300x | Pistol-grip warehouse | **DT40 + gun grip / RT40** |
| MC9300 / MC9400 | Heavy warehouse, cold chain | **RT40 full-rugged** |
| TC8300 | Gun form factor | **DT40 + gun grip** |

**Positioning notes:**
- **DT40 is the main TC-range answer** (price, warranty, Android, plus 4G and pistol-grip variants).
- **RT40 bridges consumer ↔ rugged** — best for TC15/16/21/22 customers who don't need full rugged, and steps up to full-rugged for the heavy MC9300/MC9400 class.
- **DT50 covers the TC52/57/58 mid-tier** and adds mPOS credibility (Dreamtec).
- **Mobile printers:** Urovo also has a mobile **printer** line (SP319 / SP409, plus K329/K419) positioned to replace the **Zebra ZQ610**. **Caveat: ZPL support on these needs SDK work — it is not out-of-the-box.** Pre-qualify any customer with an existing ZPL stack before quoting a Urovo mobile printer against a Zebra one.
- **Always validate first:** confirm Android LTS and accessory parity per model before pitching enterprise accounts. Some Zebra models in the wider matrix are EOL — match against current Urovo gen.

> Reference comparison live on the IOR website: **Urovo RT40S vs Zebra MC3300x** — RT40S wins on OS currency (Android 13→17 vs 10), Wi-Fi 6 vs Wi-Fi 5, IP68 vs IP64, and integrated 4G; MC3300x retains an edge on raw battery capacity (7,000 mAh) and 21.4 m extended-range scanning.
