# Honeywell — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are)

Honeywell is one of the longest-established names in barcode scanning. The Honeywell AIDC (Automatic Identification & Data Capture) line grew out of the acquisitions of Metrologic and Hand Held Products (HHP), which is why you still see those heritage product families in the catalogue — Voyager and Orbit (ex-Metrologic) and Xenon/Granit (ex-HHP). The brand is recognised globally and carries strong credibility with end users and IT buyers who have run Honeywell scanners for years.

For IO Resource, Honeywell is effectively a **barcode scanner brand** — close to 100% of what IOR sells under Honeywell is handheld and presentation scanners, with a small amount of mobile-computer and accessory volume. It is a current **preferred line**: when a customer asks for Honeywell by name, or already runs a Honeywell fleet they want to extend, IOR quotes and supplies it as a first-class product.

Market position: Honeywell sits alongside Zebra at the premium / enterprise end of the scanner market, with a deep range that spans entry-level retail POS up to ultra-rugged industrial. IOR carries them because they are a trusted, broadly-compatible brand with proven scan-engine performance, a wide form-factor range, and an installed base across the reseller's customers — particularly the customers being inherited from the departing rep (~7.5% of that book), where continuity matters.

## Product line (key SKUs)

**Voyager 1200g** — Entry-level single-line laser scanner. 1D only, corded USB, lightweight (~100g), 100 scan lines/sec, 1.5m drop spec, IP42. Plug-and-play, no drivers. Use case: lowest-cost handheld for retail POS / general 1D scanning.

**Voyager 1400g** — General-duty 2D area-imaging scanner. 1D **and** 2D, corded USB, omnidirectional, reads barcodes off phone screens, 1.5m drop, IP42. The "XP Curve" (1400G XP / 1400GSR) variant adds an improved window/optics. Use case: affordable step-up from laser when you need 2D and mobile-coupon reading at the counter.

**Voyager 1202g** — Cordless version of the 1200g family. 1D laser, Bluetooth with charge cradle, corded-free at the POS. Use case: retail counters where the cable gets in the way.

**Voyager XP 1470g / 1472g** — General-duty area-imaging scanner, "XP" generation with upgraded optics for fast, reliable reads on damaged or low-contrast codes. 1D/2D. The **1472g is the cordless (Bluetooth) 2D kit** (1470g is the corded sibling). Use case: the workhorse general-purpose 2D scanner — counters, back-office, light warehouse.

**Voyager 1952g** — Wireless 2D area imager. Bluetooth with charge cradle, ~13-hour / 50,000-scan battery, reads phone screens, omnidirectional, 1.5m drop, IP42. Use case: a full shift of cordless 2D scanning at retail / trade counters.

**Xenon 1950g** — Premium handheld 2D area imager (HHP heritage). 1D/2D, corded or cordless variants, strong motion tolerance and depth of field, reads dense and damaged codes. Use case: high-throughput retail, healthcare and light-industrial where read performance matters.

**Orbit (MS7120 / 7190g)** — Hands-free **presentation** scanners. MS7120 Orbit is the classic 1D omnidirectional laser presentation unit; the **7190g** is the modern hybrid 1D/2D presentation scanner that also reads phone screens and supports handheld use for bulky items. Use case: fixed-position checkout where staff sweep items past the scanner.

**Eclipse MS5145** — Compact single-line 1D laser handheld with CodeGate (target-and-trigger) and CodeSense (wake-on-scan). 72 lines/sec, ~100g, USB/RS232/wedge, 3-year warranty. Use case: menu/line-item scanning and value retail POS.

**Genesis (7580g)** — Compact area-imaging **presentation** scanner. 1D/2D, hands-free with handheld option, small counter footprint. Use case: space-constrained POS that still needs 2D / phone-screen reading.

**Granit 1990i / 1991i (1980i predecessor)** — Ultra-rugged industrial 2D area imager. 1D/2D, IP65, sealed for dust and water, reads damaged/dirty/poorly-printed labels, high motion tolerance, **wide decode range** (close-up to long-range, including the "XR" extended-range variant for warehouse rack labels). Corded or cordless. Use case: warehouse, manufacturing and distribution where scanners take a beating.

**HF680** — Fixed-mount / hands-free 2D imager designed for fast presentation reading, including high-volume retail and ID/coupon scanning. Use case: embedded or counter-mount hands-free 2D.

**Mobile computers** — A small part of the IOR Honeywell mix. Honeywell's enterprise handheld families (e.g. the CT and CK Android series) are available, but scanner volume dominates; lead with scanners and treat mobile computers as available-on-request rather than the headline.

## Where it fits (verticals)

- **Retail / hospitality POS** — the core IOR Honeywell vertical. Voyager 1200g/1400g/1202g handhelds, Orbit and Genesis presentation scanners, Eclipse for value tills.
- **Logistics / warehouse / distribution** — Granit ultra-rugged handhelds (incl. XR extended range for rack labels), Xenon for general handling.
- **Manufacturing** — Granit and Xenon on the line and in goods-in, where labels are damaged or dirty.
- **Healthcare** — Xenon and Voyager imagers for patient ID, specimen and medication barcodes; disinfectant-ready variants exist in the wider Honeywell range.
- **Field / mobile** — Honeywell mobile computers where a customer specifically wants Android handhelds (smaller part of the IOR mix).

## Sales talking points

1. **Established brand the customer already trusts.** Honeywell (with its Metrologic and Hand Held Products heritage) has one of the largest installed bases in scanning — easy to recommend when a customer wants a known name or is extending an existing fleet.
2. **A form factor for every job.** One brand covers entry laser (1200g/Eclipse), general-duty 2D (1400g/1472g), cordless (1202g/1952g/1472g), premium handheld (Xenon), presentation (Orbit/Genesis/HF680) and ultra-rugged (Granit). You can standardise a whole site on one vendor.
3. **Proven scan-engine performance.** Honeywell imagers handle damaged, low-contrast and phone-screen barcodes well, with strong motion tolerance and depth of field — fewer mis-reads, faster throughput.
4. **Drop-in compatibility.** USB plug-and-play, OPOS/JPOS support and the usual interface options (USB, RS232, keyboard wedge) mean Honeywell scanners slot into virtually any POS or ERP without driver hassle.
5. **Ruggedness when it's needed.** IP65 Granit and solid drop specs across the range stand up to warehouse and industrial use, reducing replacement cycles.

## Common objections + answers

**"Honeywell is expensive compared to budget scanners."**
You're buying reliability and a deep, supported range. For high-volume environments the lower mis-read rate and longer service life pay back quickly. Where budget is the deciding factor, IOR can also position a value alternative — but for a known-good standard, Honeywell earns its price.

**"We already run a Zebra / mixed fleet — why switch?"**
You don't have to rip and replace. Honeywell scanners are interface-compatible and can be introduced alongside existing kit. Where a customer wants to consolidate suppliers, IOR can map their current models to Honeywell (or Datalogic) equivalents.

**"Will it read codes off phone screens / loyalty apps?"**
Yes — any of the 2D area imagers (Voyager 1400g/1472g/1952g, Xenon, Orbit 7190g, Genesis, HF680) read mobile-screen barcodes reliably. Only the entry single-line laser units (1200g, Eclipse) are 1D-paper-only.

**"Our environment is harsh — will it survive?"**
For warehouse/industrial, the Granit range is IP65-sealed, built for drops and reads dirty/damaged labels; the XR variant scans rack labels at distance. For everyday retail, the Voyager line's 1.5m drop spec is plenty.

**"Is this an end-of-life or being-phased-out line?"**
No — Honeywell is a current, supported, preferred line that IOR actively quotes and stocks-to-order. The product families (Voyager, Orbit, Xenon, Granit) are all in production with current accessories, spares and warranty.

## Competitive / displacement angle

*Internal note for the rep — kept neutral.* Honeywell, Datalogic and Zebra are the three established scanner brands IOR works with. All three are credible; the differences are in range emphasis and IOR's own go-to.

- **vs Datalogic:** Datalogic is IOR's strategic scanner push and the brand to lead with on new, open opportunities — it gives IOR the best position commercially and a directly comparable range (e.g. Magellan presentation scanners vs Orbit; QuickScan/Gryphon handhelds vs Voyager/Xenon). Honeywell is the **steady, established alternative**: reach for it when a customer asks for Honeywell by name, is extending a Honeywell fleet, or values the brand recognition. Both read 1D/2D and phone screens equally well; positioning is about fit and continuity, not capability gaps.
- **vs Zebra:** Honeywell and Zebra are close peers at the premium end, with very similar capability and price tiers. Where a customer is open, IOR generally has a stronger position offering Datalogic or Honeywell than Zebra. Against Zebra specifically, Honeywell's heritage range and broad form-factor coverage make it an easy like-for-like swap.

Bottom line for the rep: **honour Honeywell when the customer wants it** (especially inherited Honeywell accounts), and **introduce Datalogic on greenfield opportunities** where there's no existing brand preference.
