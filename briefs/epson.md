# Epson — Training Brief

## Positioning (who they are)

Epson (Seiko Epson Corporation, Japan) is the global market leader in point-of-sale receipt printing. Its TM-series ("TM" = Thermal Matrix/Tally Machine heritage) thermal and impact printers are the de-facto industry standard — IDC ranks Epson No.1 for POS receipt printers in Western Europe, and millions of units are deployed worldwide. The ESC/POS command language Epson created is itself the universal POS print standard that virtually every till, EPOS package and payment platform supports out of the box.

For IO Resource, Epson is a strategic, high-priority brand: roughly 5.6% of total IOR revenue and the No.2 line in the departing rep's book (~20% of that book). IOR carries Epson because it is the safe, ubiquitous default for any retail or hospitality POS deal — a brand customers ask for by name, that "just works" with their POS software, and that carries the lowest support and integration risk. IOR also offers spares and a repair-centre service for the range, which deepens the after-sales relationship.

Key ecosystem points a rep should know:
- **ESC/POS** — Epson's command set, the universal POS print language. Compatibility is rarely a question with Epson.
- **ePOS-Print / ePOS-Device / Server Direct Print** — Epson's built-in technologies for printing direct from tablets, browsers and web/cloud POS apps without a PC driver.
- **Driver breadth** — APD, OPOS, JavaPOS, Virtual Port, Linux CUPS, macOS, plus iOS/Android SDKs.

## Product line (key SKUs)

### Epson TM-T88VII — flagship 80mm thermal receipt printer
- Print method: thermal line; **500 mm/s** max print speed; 180 dpi.
- Interfaces: USB 2.0 (Type-A host + Type-B), Ethernet (10/100), RS-232 serial, drawer kick-out; optional Wi-Fi via OT-WL06 dongle.
- Paper: 80mm (and 58mm), 83mm max roll diameter.
- Other: ePOS-Print / Server Direct Print built in; backwards-compatible with T88V/T88VI software and footprint; Phenol-free paper support; 48-month carry-in warranty.
- Use case: the premium counter receipt printer for any busy retail or hospitality till where speed and reliability matter most.

### Epson TM-T20IV — entry-level / value 80mm thermal receipt printer
- Print method: thermal line; **250 mm/s**; 203 dpi.
- Interfaces: dual model (USB + serial) or triple model (USB + serial + Ethernet + USB host); optional Wi-Fi via OT-WL06.
- Paper: 80mm or 58mm; paper-saving features cut paper use up to ~30%.
- Other: ESC/POS, ePOS technology, drives mPOS and PC-POS; 2 million-cut auto-cutter; status LED.
- Use case: the affordable, reliable workhorse for SME retail and hospitality POS where budget matters but the Epson name is still wanted.

### Epson TM-m30III — compact mPOS / tablet POS receipt printer
- Print method: thermal; **300 mm/s**; tiny 127 x 127 x 127mm cube, 1.3 kg; IPX2.
- Interfaces: USB-C (PD 18W to charge/power a tablet), USB-B, Ethernet, **integrated Bluetooth and Wi-Fi**; 1x USB host; microSD (fiscal).
- Paper: 80mm; partial or full cut; de-curl; both-orientation near-end sensor.
- Other: charge + data + network tethering to a tablet over one USB-C cable; iOS/Android/Windows.
- Use case: modern tablet/cloud POS and mPOS counters — cafés, boutiques, quick-service — where a small, wireless, tablet-friendly printer is needed.

### Epson TM-L90II — linerless label + receipt printer
- Print method: thermal; **linerless-compatible** (prints labels and receipts from one device).
- Interfaces: USB, Ethernet, Serial.
- Paper: 80mm thermal, linerless.
- Other: compact footprint; cuts waste and cost by removing label liner backing.
- Use case: hospitality order/kitchen tickets and shelf/sticky labels from a single device — strong in food-to-go, grocery and order-routing setups.

### Epson TM-U220 — 9-pin impact (dot-matrix) kitchen printer
- Print method: **9-pin dot matrix (impact)**; up to 6 lines/sec at 30 cpl (4.7 lps at 40 cpl); two-colour red/black via ERC-38 ribbon.
- Interfaces: RS-232 / parallel / USB / Ethernet (interface-dependent); dual cash-drawer kick-out.
- Paper: 58 / 70 / 76mm widths (Type B & D); drop-in loading; ERC-38 ribbon.
- Variants: **A** (auto-cutter + journal), **B** (auto-cutter + near-end sensor), **D** (basic).
- Use case: hot, steamy kitchens and bars — impact printing survives heat/grease where thermal fades, and red highlighting flags priority orders. Current model is the TM-U220II.

### Epson TM-P-series (TM-P20II / TM-P80II) — portable / mobile receipt printers
- Print method: thermal; 100 mm/s; battery-powered; **IP54** rated, drop-resistant (P20II to ~2.1m).
- Interfaces: USB-C; optional integrated Bluetooth and Wi-Fi (Wi-Fi Direct to up to 8 devices); in-vehicle approved.
- Paper: 58mm (P20II, 210g) or 80mm (P80II, 385g).
- Battery: up to ~25h Wi-Fi / ~47h Bluetooth (P-series II).
- Use case: mobile/queue-busting receipts, delivery, market stalls, in-van invoicing, line-busting in retail.

### Epson DS-730N — networked document scanner
- A4 sheet-fed network document scanner (Workforce range). Sits alongside the printer line for back-office capture; a useful attach in office/retail-admin deals. (Not a POS printer — context only.)

## Where it fits (verticals)

- **Retail** — counter POS receipts: convenience, grocery, fashion, forecourt, health & beauty. T88VII (premium tills), T20IV (value/SME). Epson printers are also the standard receipt module inside Pan Oston self-checkout units IOR supplies (TM-T88).
- **Hospitality** — front-of-house receipts (T88/T20/m30III) and back-of-house kitchen tickets (TM-U220 impact, TM-L90II linerless order tickets). Pubs, restaurants, QSR, cafés, hotels.
- **mPOS / tablet & cloud POS** — TM-m30III for tablet-based and cloud EPOS (square-style counters, pop-ups, boutiques).
- **Mobile / field** — TM-P20II/P80II for delivery, markets, events, in-vehicle.
- Typical customer: any business taking card/cash payments that needs a printed or labelled output — from a single-till independent up to multi-site retail/hospitality chains and the EPOS resellers/ISVs who specify hardware for them.

## Sales talking points

1. **Market leader / ubiquity** — "Epson is the No.1 receipt printer brand; it's the safe default and customers ask for it by name." Lowest integration and support risk.
2. **It just works with the POS software** — ESC/POS is Epson's own standard; virtually every till, EPOS package and payment terminal supports Epson natively. No driver hunting, no compatibility surprises.
3. **Proven reliability** — high MTBF/MCBF and millions of deployed units; the T88 flagship is "tried and trusted" build quality. Less downtime = the real cost saving for the customer.
4. **Connectivity for every setup** — USB, serial, Ethernet, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and USB-C across the range, so there's an Epson for legacy PC-POS through to modern tablet/cloud POS.
5. **Whole-of-range + service** — one brand covers counter, kitchen, label, mobile and self-checkout, and IOR backs it with spares and a repair centre. Easy to standardise a customer's estate on one brand.

## Common objections + answers

- **"Epson is more expensive than the cheap no-name / Bixolon unit."**
  Lead on total cost of ownership: higher reliability and the lowest integration risk mean fewer failures, fewer support calls and no software headaches. And IOR has a genuine value tier — the **TM-T20IV** — that competes on price while keeping the Epson name and ESC/POS compatibility.

- **"We're moving to tablet / cloud POS — is Epson still relevant?"**
  Very much so. The **TM-m30III** is purpose-built for tablet POS: USB-C power and tethering, integrated Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, tiny cube footprint, and Epson ePOS-Print for direct printing from web/cloud apps. Mobile? The **TM-P-series** prints from a phone or tablet over Bluetooth/Wi-Fi.

- **"Thermal receipts fade in our kitchen / hot environment."**
  Correct — that's why we'd spec the **TM-U220 impact** printer for kitchens. Dot-matrix impact survives heat, steam and grease, and the two-colour ribbon highlights priority orders. Use thermal (T88/T20) at the front counter and impact at the pass.

- **"Can it do labels as well as receipts so we don't need two devices?"**
  Yes — the **TM-L90II** is linerless-compatible and prints both labels and receipts from one unit, cutting both hardware and consumable waste.

- **"We already have an estate of older Epson printers."**
  Ideal — the **TM-T88VII is backwards-compatible with T88V/T88VI software and shares the footprint**, so a refresh needs no software changes and no till/counter rework. Same story across the T20 family. Easy, low-risk upgrade conversation.

## Competitive / displacement angle

On **receipt printers**, Epson is the incumbent market leader and IOR's lead brand — this is usually a *defend/standardise* play, not a displacement play. Position against the two main challengers:

- **vs Star Micronics** — Star is the credible No.2, strong in cloud/tablet POS bundles (e.g. mC-Print, TSP100/TSP650, often pushed by ISVs). Counter: Epson's broader install base and ESC/POS ubiquity mean lower compatibility risk; the T88VII out-specs typical Star counter units on top-end print speed (500 mm/s); and Epson's range depth (impact, linerless, mobile, self-checkout) lets a customer standardise on one brand rather than mixing.
- **vs Bixolon** — Bixolon (Korean) competes hard on price and is a strong label-printer brand in IOR's portfolio (part of the "Kill the Zebra" labelling strategy). On *receipt* printers, hold the line with Epson: superior brand recognition, the ESC/POS standard, proven reliability and POS-software certification breadth. Where the deal is purely price-led at the entry level, the **TM-T20IV** is IOR's Epson answer to Bixolon — keeps the trusted brand without overpaying.

Rule of thumb for a new rep: **lead with Epson on receipt printers; reach for Bixolon/Toshiba on labels.** Don't let a low-cost challenger commoditise a receipt-printer deal — sell reliability, compatibility and the value-tier T20IV.
